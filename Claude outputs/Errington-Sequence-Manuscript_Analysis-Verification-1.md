# Verification of analyses — "Ordinal position in auditory sequences is encoded in population dynamics in macaque frontal cortex"

Reviewed: manuscript PDF against the analysis code in `2024-aglt-sequence` (folders `analysis/1_population`, `2_singleunit`, `3_periodicity`, `4_timescale`, and `data-extraction`). Each script was read directly and checked against the Methods text and the specific numbers reported in Results/Figures 2–4. Findings below are separated into **CONFIRMED** (verified by direct reading of the code) and **PLAUSIBLE** (a real code issue whose downstream numerical impact couldn't be independently quantified without re-running the pipeline on the actual data).

## Bottom line

The overall dissociation the paper reports — frontal cortex trajectories evolve progressively across a sequence while auditory cortex resets each element, with correspondingly better position decoding, longer intrinsic timescales, and weaker periodicity in frontal cortex — is implemented by real, mostly correctly-built analysis code, not fabricated. However, I found several issues that range from cosmetic to serious enough that they could materially change specific reported numbers. The two I'd flag as needing attention before submission/resubmission are the LDA decoding leakage (Fig 2C–D) and the clustering chi-square bug (Fig 3, the χ²=0.01 result). Everything else is lower stakes but worth cleaning up.

**Update:** the LDA decoding leakage flagged below was followed up with an actual leak-free re-analysis (see "Follow-up: rigorous re-analysis of Figure 2 decoding" after this section). Across three independent, leak-free, trial-level re-implementations, the paper's central claim — that frontal cortex decodes ordinal position significantly better than auditory cortex — did not replicate. In two of the three, frontal position decoding was numerically *lower* than auditory's, and in none was the frontal-vs-auditory difference statistically significant.

**Second update, more serious:** follow-up work traced the whole-sequence-anchored numbers to a large sequence-context/trial-carryover confound (population activity, especially frontal, separates trials by upcoming/current sequence group even from a physically identical stimulus — resolved to be genuine carryover from the *previous* trial, not anticipation). That confound turns out to structurally threaten the position- and identity-decoding claims themselves, not just the whole-sequence variant, because with only 4 fixed sequences, position/identity labels are not independent of sequence identity (position 4 and 2 of the 3 position-5 identity classes are *total* aliases for sequence group). A structural control that removes this confound by construction — training the position classifier on one pair of sequences and testing on the other — found **no generalizable ordinal-position decoding above chance in either area, and no frontal advantage** (auditory 0.367 vs. chance 0.333, p=0.289; frontal exactly at chance, p=0.512; frontal vs. auditory p=0.647). This is now the most consequential finding in this review: it suggests that most of the Figure 2C–D decoding evidence, across every variant tested, reflects sequence-context/carryover information rather than genuine stimulus-order coding, and needs to be resolved — including checking whether the paper's other main line of evidence, the continuous PC-trajectory/linearity result, survives the same control — before Figure 2's claims and framing are treated as reliable.

**Third update:** a methodologically independent RSA multiple-regression re-analysis (partial correlation rather than classification) confirms the position null a third time, and additionally finds that the one confound-controlled, statistically robust identity signal in the data runs in the *opposite* direction to the paper's claim — significantly stronger in auditory cortex than frontal (p<0.001) — while frontal cortex's one genuine unique advantage is a small residual sequence-group (context) signal, not position or identity. See "A structurally independent triangulation" under Figure 2 below.

## Figure 2 — Population trajectories and decoding

**1. Timepoint-level (not trial-level) LDA decoding, with random-not-block train/test splitting — SERIOUS, CONFIRMED.**
In `pca_lda_id_position.m`, each "sample" fed to the LDA classifier is a single timepoint of a heavily smoothed (100 ms moving average), condition-averaged PC trajectory — not an independent trial — and `cvpartition(labels,'Holdout',0.3)` splits these timepoints uniformly at random rather than in contiguous blocks. Adjacent timepoints are near-duplicates, so this routinely puts near-identical samples on both sides of the train/test split. This is a real leakage mechanism and a plausible explanation for the very high reported accuracies (94.84% frontal position, 99.84% frontal identity). The manuscript text doesn't disclose that decoding operates on trajectory timepoints rather than trials, which readers would likely assume.

**2. Bootstrap "70/30" split isn't actually 70/30 — MODERATE, CONFIRMED.**
The bootstrap loop draws the training set via `randsample(n, round(0.7*n), true)` — *with* replacement — which by the coupon-collector effect yields only ≈50% unique training indices, not 70%. The held-out "test" set is therefore ≈50% of samples, not 30%. (The separate single-shot `cvpartition` holdout elsewhere in the file *is* a true 70/30 split.)

**3. `get_xcond_pca.m` only intersects the first two conditions' valid-neuron sets, not all of them — SERIOUS, CONFIRMED (impact PLAUSIBLE).** The manuscript states "only neurons with valid responses in all conditions were retained." The code computes `intersect(valid_idx{1}, valid_idx{2})` regardless of whether 3 or 4 conditions are pooled (3 for identity, 4 for position). If any neuron has missing data in condition 3 or 4 only, its NaNs would still enter the joint PCA matrix, and MATLAB's default listwise-deletion in `pca()` could silently drop an entire timepoint block for the whole population. Whether this actually bites depends on the real missing-data pattern, which I couldn't check without loading the raw data.

**4. Minor issues:** a trial-count sanity-check variable is mislabeled (checks position 4 when identity decoding actually draws from position 5) but appears unused for any filtering, so it's inert; the shuffled-control R² analysis is correctly implemented (per-neuron `randperm` time-shuffle before PCA, matching the text) but there's a second, differently-smoothed shuffled variable that's built and never used (dead code); Procrustes similarity is computed for only 3 of the 4 consecutive element pairs (element-5's segment is built but not compared), while the onset-distance metric correctly uses all 4 pairs; no script saves results to a file — all headline numbers are read off console output, which is a reproducibility gap rather than a correctness bug.

**Confirmed correct:** the ±10 ms onset-window, the 4-consecutive-pair distance metric, Procrustes with scaling disabled, the chance levels (1/3 for identity via C/F/G, 1/4 for position via positions 2–5 — matching the design described in the text, not a naive 1/5), and the general bootstrap-comparison function (`bootstrap_compare.m`) all match the methods exactly. The PC1-vs-PC2-3 comparison (Δ=15.7%) comes from a genuine parallel sweep script, not an ad hoc number.

## Follow-up: rigorous re-analysis of Figure 2 decoding (leak-free, trial-level)

Finding #1 above (timepoint-level samples, leaky train/test split) was serious enough to warrant an actual re-run rather than just a written flag. Three new, self-contained scripts were built and run against the real data, all in `analysis/1_population/`, all using genuine single-trial samples, bootstrapped pseudo-populations (neurons weren't all recorded simultaneously), PCA refit on the training fold only inside each cross-validation fold (never on data the classifier is later tested on), repeated stratified 5-fold CV nested inside a 300-iteration trial-resampling bootstrap, and a 300-iteration label-permutation test for chance level (rather than only comparing to the theoretical 1/3 or 1/4):

- **`pca_lda_id_position_rigorous.m`** — features are single-trial, baseline z-scored firing rates in the same 0–413 ms post-element-onset window the original script used, re-referenced to each element's own local context (matching the original design, minus the leakage). Two feature variants were tried: a flat window-average (one scalar per trial per neuron), and 4 time-bins per trial (~103 ms each) to give any within-window temporal shape a chance to contribute, since the manuscript's own trajectory finding implies position information may live in the evolving *shape* of the response, not its average level.
- **`pca_lda_id_position_wholeseq_rigorous.m`** — same leak-free machinery, but features are sliced directly out of the continuous, sequence-onset-aligned trace (`sdf.sequenceOnset`, the same data source behind the Fig 2A/B trajectory/linearity result) at each element's fixed absolute onset time (0/563/1126/1688/2252 ms), using one global pre-sequence baseline for the whole trial rather than re-referencing at each element. This tests whether cross-element drift dynamics — which the element-epoched representation structurally cannot see — recover stronger position decoding.
- **`sequence_confound_check.m`** — a diagnostic: decodes *which of the 4 grammatical sequences* a trial belongs to, using only the position-1 window, where the stimulus (the sound "A") is physically identical across all four sequences. Any above-chance decoding here has no possible acoustic explanation and reveals non-specific, trial/context-level information riding along in the population signal.

**Results** (chance = 0.333 for 3-way identity, 0.25 for 4-way position/sequence; all p-values are permutation p-values against 300 label-shuffled null iterations):

| Analysis | Identity: Auditory / Frontal | Position: Auditory / Frontal | Frontal vs auditory (position) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Original script (leaky) | 92.03% / 99.84% | 58.57% / 94.84% | p < 0.001 (frontal higher) |
| Rigorous, element-epoched, flat mean | 0.861 / 0.867 | 0.517 / 0.383 | p = 0.200, frontal *lower* |
| Rigorous, element-epoched, 4 time-bins | 0.983 / 0.983 | 0.704 / 0.465 | p = 0.253, frontal *lower* |
| Rigorous, whole-sequence-anchored | 1.000 / 0.994 (see caveat) | 0.700 / 0.660 | p = 0.800, no difference |
| Confound check (sequence ID from identical "A" stimulus) | — | 0.673 / 0.892 (chance 0.25) | frontal much higher — a real, separate context/drift signal, not position or identity coding |

**Interpretation.** Both areas decode position modestly above chance in every leak-free variant, so position information isn't literally absent from either population. But the paper's specific, headline claim — that frontal cortex decodes position *significantly better than* auditory cortex — did not survive removing the leakage in any of the three independent re-implementations; in two of three, frontal was nominally *lower*, not higher, than auditory. The whole-sequence-anchored variant's near-ceiling identity numbers should not be taken at face value: the confound check shows that representation carries substantial non-acoustic, trial-level context information (and considerably more of it in frontal, 89% vs 67% decodable from a physically identical stimulus), which is very likely inflating those numbers rather than reflecting genuine sound-identity tuning.

**Control for the confound (`trialorder_confound_check.m`).** That frontal-vs-auditory context signal survived an important control: pooling all 4,276 nonviolation trials across 79 sessions, within-session trial order (elapsed time in the recording) did *not* predict which of the 4 sequences was played (nested-CV accuracy 0.256 vs. permutation-null median 0.256, 95th percentile 0.267; p = 0.465) — so sequence randomization looks intact, and simple session-time drift is ruled out as the explanation.

Two further checks ruled out the remaining mundane explanations. **`session_group_confound_check.m`** confirmed the 4 sequence groups are quota-balanced within essentially every one of the 79 sessions (χ²(234) = 47.66, p ≈ 1, both monkeys individually), so the effect isn't an artefact of uneven group representation across sessions or animals. **`prevtrial_confound_check.m`** then found that the trial-generation design isn't fully independent draw-to-draw: across 4,197 consecutive nonviolation trial pairs, the previous trial's group predicts the current trial's group far better than chance (χ²(9) = 77.20, p = 5.8×10⁻¹³), with an "avoid immediate repeat" structure (observed same-group repeat rate 0.161 vs. 0.250 expected under independence). That made trial-N−1 carryover a statistically live candidate mechanism.

**Resolution (`prevtrial_neural_check.m`).** Direct neural test: instead of decoding the *current* trial's group from the position-1 window (identical "A" stimulus in all four sequences), this decodes the *previous* trial's group from that same current-trial window. If the original confound were carryover rather than anticipation, this should decode at least as well as the original — and it does, decisively: **auditory 0.854 (vs. 0.673 for current-trial group), frontal 0.985 (vs. 0.892 for current-trial group)**, both far above the 0.25 chance level and above every permutation-null iteration (p = 0.0033, the floor given 300 permutations). Previous-trial identity is *more* decodable from this window than current-trial identity is — exactly the pattern expected if the current-trial decode is entirely secondary, riding on residual state from the trial before, attenuated only by the imperfect (repeat-suppressed, not deterministic) statistical link between consecutive trials' groups documented above. There is no need to invoke anticipatory/predictive coding of the upcoming sequence to explain any of this.

So the full chain resolves cleanly: the population signal at trial onset does not know what sequence is about to play — it still reflects which sequence played *last*, more strongly and more persistently in frontal cortex than in auditory cortex, and because the trial-order design mildly discourages immediate repeats, that lingering state is statistically informative about the upcoming trial's group too, without any prediction being involved. This is consistent with (and arguably a nice corollary of) the paper's own Figure 4 finding that frontal cortex has longer intrinsic timescales than auditory cortex — it's the same area-level asymmetry in persistence, just visible here as *inter*-trial carryover rather than *intra*-trial dynamics. It does mean the whole-sequence-anchored decoding numbers in the table above (and any future analysis anchored to whole-trial or sequence-onset time bases without controlling for trial history) should not be read as evidence of genuine stimulus/identity tuning without first accounting for this carryover.

**Structural re-examination of the position-decoding claim itself, and a decisive negative test (`position_crossgroup_generalization.m`).** The carryover/context confound isn't confined to the diagnostic — it structurally threatens the manuscript's position-decoding claim too, because there are only 4 grammatical sequences and "which ordinal position is C at" is not independent of "which sequence is this": letter C at position 4 occurs *only* in one specific sequence (a total, unfixable confound, dropped from this analysis), and at position 5, identity F and identity G are each *only* ever produced by one specific sequence (meaning the manuscript's identity-decoding claim at position 5 cannot be rigorously dissociated from sequence-identity decoding in this design at all — 2 of its 3 classes are pure aliases for sequence group). Positions 2, 3 and 5, however, are each produced by exactly two different sequences, which allows a structural (not statistical) control: split the 4 sequences into two pairs, train the position classifier on one pair, test on the other (and vice versa). Since train and test trials come from entirely different physical sequences, no sequence-identity or carryover signal can transfer across the split — only genuine, sequence-independent ordinal-position coding can generalize.

The result was a clean, well-powered null in both areas: auditory 0.367 (chance 0.333, 95% CI [0.300, 0.500], permutation p = 0.289), frontal exactly at chance (0.333, 95% CI [0.244, 0.422], permutation p = 0.512), and no frontal-vs-auditory difference (Δ = −0.033%, p = 0.647, frontal numerically *lower* if anything). Once the sequence-identity shortcut is structurally removed, there is no detectable, generalizable ordinal-position code in either area by this (categorical, LDA-classification) measure — let alone a frontal advantage. Combined with the identity-decoding structural confound above, this means the bulk of the manuscript's Fig 2C–D decoding numbers, across every variant tested so far (original, rigorous element-epoched, rigorous whole-sequence-anchored), are best explained by sequence-context/carryover information leaking into a design that cannot fully separate it from genuine stimulus-order coding, rather than by real ordinal position or identity tuning.

This does *not* by itself rule out every form of evidence for frontal sequential structure in the paper: Fig 2A/B's trajectory/linearity result (PC1 R² against time-in-sequence, `pca_seq_main.m`) is a different, continuous operationalization — regression against elapsed time rather than classification of discrete position bins — and has not yet been tested against the same sequence-identity confound. That is the one remaining piece of evidence for genuine within-trial ordinal structure that hasn't been checked, and given everything found so far, it should be checked with an equivalent cross-sequence-group control before being trusted as-is.

**Trajectory/linearity result under the same control (`pca_seq_linearity_crossgroup.m`) — partially survives, but at a much smaller effect size and with no frontal-specific advantage.** Unlike position/identity decoding, the manuscript's linearity analysis pools all 4 sequences per neuron before PCA, so it isn't confounded by sequence-identity in the same direct way — but the same structural control still applies: fit the PC1 axis on one pair of sequences (Set A), project the *other*, entirely disjoint pair (Set B) onto it, and see whether the temporal relationship survives (and vice versa, averaged).

It partially does. Frontal PC1 cross-group R² = 0.172 (95% CI [0.007, 0.656]), significantly above a time-shuffled null (median null R² ≈ 0.001, p = 0.013). Auditory PC1 cross-group R² = 0.125 (95% CI [0.002, 0.345]), borderline against the same null (p = 0.053). So — in contrast to the categorical decoding result, which collapsed completely to chance — there is a real, statistically defensible, sequence-independent temporal signal in at least frontal cortex (auditory is suggestive but not quite significant). That said, two things temper this: first, the effect size has shrunk drastically from the reported whole-pool R² of ~0.76 down to ~0.17 once only two disjoint sequences are used and the fitted axis has to generalize out-of-sample — even the same-set (non-generalizing, fit-and-tested-on-the-same-two-sequences) R² was already only 0.340 for frontal and 0.154 for auditory, roughly half the reported magnitude, before cross-group testing shrank it further. Second, and most relevant to the specific claim in question, **frontal's cross-group R² was not significantly higher than auditory's (0.172 vs. 0.125, p = 0.667)** — so while some genuine temporal/ordinal structure does appear to survive rigorous, confound-controlled testing (making this analysis line more defensible than the discrete decoding one), it does not support a frontal-specific advantage either.

**A structurally independent triangulation: RSA multiple regression (`rsa_position_identity_regression.m`).** Every analysis above is some form of classification or train/test generalization. As a genuinely different check — regression rather than classification, partial correlation rather than held-out accuracy — this script built all 16 (sequence-group × position 2–5) cells per neuron, computed the single-trial pairwise pattern-dissimilarity matrix within a bootstrapped pseudo-population (762/798 auditory and 664/676 frontal neurons passed the stricter ≥3-trials-in-all-16-cells criterion), and regressed dissimilarity simultaneously on same-position, same-identity, and same-sequence-group predictors — so each predictor's coefficient reflects its *unique* contribution once the other two (including sequence-group, the source of the confound) are held constant in the same model, rather than removed by which trials happen to enter training vs. test.

The results corroborate the null on position and add two new findings. **Position:** neither area shows a unique position effect (auditory true-vs-null diff ≈ 0, p = 0.400; frontal ≈ 0, p = 0.913), and frontal is not different from auditory (p = 0.687) — a third, methodologically independent confirmation of the cross-group decoding null above. **Identity:** auditory shows a robust, significant unique identity effect (true beta −0.027 vs. null −0.013, p < 0.001) that frontal does not (true beta −0.012 vs. null −0.012, p = 0.553) — and the frontal-vs-auditory comparison on this coefficient is itself highly significant (p < 0.001), but in the *opposite* direction to the paper's claim: auditory's unique identity coding is stronger than frontal's, not weaker. **Sequence-group:** frontal shows a small but significant unique same-group effect after controlling for both position and identity (true beta −0.020 vs. null −0.018, p = 0.040); auditory does not (p = 0.820). In other words, once position and identity are partialled out, what's left over that frontal cortex reliably encodes is *which specific sequence this is* — a categorical/contextual code, not an ordinal or item-identity one — which fits naturally with the trial-N−1 carryover and longer intrinsic-timescale findings elsewhere in this review: frontal cortex appears to carry more sequence/context-level state than auditory cortex, but that is a different claim from "frontal decodes ordinal position or identity better than auditory."

**Where this leaves the paper's central claim, across every angle tested:** the categorical position/identity decoding claim (Fig 2C-D) does not survive at all — neither the frontal-vs-auditory comparison nor detectable sequence-independent position information itself, and this is now confirmed by three independent methods (rigorous trial-level LDA, cross-sequence-group generalization, and RSA multiple regression). Identity coding at position 5 was already structurally inseparable from sequence-group in the original design; the RSA regression now shows that where a genuine, confound-controlled identity signal *does* exist, it's in auditory cortex, significantly more than in frontal — the reverse of the paper's framing. The continuous trajectory/linearity claim (Fig 2A/B) survives in much-reduced form (a real but far smaller effect, clearly significant only in frontal against a null, not against auditory). The one place frontal does show a unique, confound-controlled advantage is a small residual sequence-group (not position or identity) effect, consistent with frontal cortex carrying more inter-trial/contextual state, not more ordinal structure. This is now a framing-level problem, not a statistics-level one, and it points toward a different, more defensible story than the one in the current manuscript: frontal cortex may be distinguished by context/state persistence across trials and sequences, not by superior ordinal-position or item-identity coding within a sequence.

## Figure 3 — Single-unit GLM encoding and clustering

**5. Cluster chi-square test run on normalized proportions instead of raw counts — SERIOUS, CONFIRMED, and this is the direct explanation for the oddly perfect χ²(3)=0.01, p=0.9998 result.** In `glm_element_clustering.m`, before the chi-square test the cluster counts are normalized so each area's column sums to 1, and *that* normalized matrix (not the raw neuron counts) is passed into the chi-square function. Comparing two distributions that both trivially sum to the same total mechanically drives the test statistic toward zero regardless of whether the real underlying counts differ — this is very likely a bug, not a genuine null result. By contrast, the equivalent area × encoding-category test elsewhere in the same figure (χ²(3)=22.64, p<0.0001) correctly uses raw counts. I'd flag this one for correction and a rerun.

**6. GLM fits raw firing rates, not the z-scored/baseline-normalized SDFs the text describes — MODERATE, CONFIRMED.** In the function `archive/glm_sound_modulation.m` (note: it lives in an `archive/` folder), a z-scored version of the SDF is computed but never used — the actual regression is built from the un-normalized SDF. Separately, the baseline statistics that are computed (unused) are pooled across all trials/conditions rather than within each condition, as the Methods state. The single-unit *clustering* pipeline (a different script) does do within-condition baseline normalization correctly, so this issue is specific to the GLM significance-testing step, not the whole figure.

**7. GLM significance testing doesn't use MATLAB's `anova` function as stated — MODERATE, CONFIRMED.** The code fits two separate univariate `fitglm` models (one for identity, one for position) and applies an FDR correction (`mafdr`, Benjamini-Hochberg) across time that isn't mentioned anywhere in the Methods. This is a genuine mismatch between the described and actual procedure — arguably a defensible modification (FDR correction is a reasonable thing to do), but it needs to be disclosed and the Methods text corrected either way.

**8. Minor issues:** a donut-chart panel accidentally uses the auditory neuron count as the frontal denominator (visualization bug only, wouldn't affect the reported category counts, only a percentage shown in one panel); the four functional-category labels (facilitated/suppressed/ramping) are hand-mapped from arbitrary k-means cluster numbers — correctly covering all 13 clusters with no double-counting, but fragile if the clustering is ever rerun and label order changes.

**Confirmed correct:** the live pipeline is unambiguous (traced via the top-level analysis script) — several other "detection" scripts in the folder (`element_detect_*.m`, `glm_clustering.m`, `glm_element_clustering_old.m`) are dead/superseded code, not the source of the reported numbers. The 50 ms / 5-consecutive-bin significance threshold is implemented exactly as described, with no off-by-one error. The "both / identity-only / position-only / onset-only" classification logic is correctly mutually exclusive and exhaustive. The MDS(5-D)/k-means(k=2–20, 20 reps, silhouette-selected, refit with 50 reps) clustering pipeline matches the Methods closely.

## Figure 4 — Periodicity and intrinsic timescale

**9. The frontal-vs-auditory intrinsic-timescale bootstrap CI is not a genuine neuron-level bootstrap — SERIOUS, CONFIRMED, directly relevant to the borderline result (73 ms difference, CI 6–150 ms, p=0.034).** The bootstrap resamples *trials within each neuron* independently, but the fixed, full set of neurons is averaged together identically on every one of the 1000 iterations — neurons themselves are never resampled. This cancels out within-neuron trial noise but never propagates between-neuron variability into the confidence interval, which will tend to make the CI narrower than it should be. Given the reported CI already only barely excludes zero, this is worth re-doing with a proper neuron-resampling bootstrap before trusting the p=0.034.

**10. τ is fit with no positivity constraint — MODERATE, CONFIRMED.** `lsqcurvefit` is called with empty bounds, so nothing stops a degenerate/negative-timescale fit from occurring; this doesn't necessarily bias the reported means but is inconsistent with "initial parameter estimates optimised for convergence" implying a controlled fit.

**11. Zero-firing exclusion drops whole neurons, not individual bins — MODERATE, CONFIRMED.** The text says bins with zero mean firing should be excluded; the code instead excludes an entire neuron if *any* of its 20 baseline bins has zero mean firing, which is a stricter and different criterion than described.

**12. Duplicated periodicity code with a different, non-matching parameter — MODERATE, CONFIRMED.** The periodicity/`findpeaks` analysis is implemented twice: once in `seq_autocorr.m` (peak prominence 0.25, matching the manuscript, and the one actually used for the reported clusters/figure), and again inside `acf_intrinsic_timescales.m` with a different, undocumented prominence (0.20). The second copy appears to be an unused leftover, but its presence in the same file as the "live" timescale analysis is worth tidying up so it can't be run by mistake.

**Confirmed correct:** the pre-stimulus (intrinsic timescale, −1000 to 0 ms, 20×50ms bins) and post-stimulus (periodicity, 0–2665 ms) analyses use genuinely separate time windows with no leakage between them; `xcorr` with `'coeff'` normalization, the 50–500 ms exponential-fit window, and the R² calculation (proper 1 − SS_res/SS_tot, not a spurious correlation-squared) all match the text. I could not confirm, from this file alone, that the "significantly responsive" neuron-inclusion criterion for the timescale analysis is the same significance criterion used in Figure 3 — the relevant index variables are defined upstream and weren't traceable within this script.

## Neuron and session counts

The total N (676 frontal + 798 auditory = **1474**) matches exactly the size of the final analysis-ready data structure (`data/sound_align/sdf_soundAlign_data.mat`, confirmed to contain exactly 1474 entries), and the session count (79 = 31 monkey T + 48 monkey W) matches exactly against the raw spike log. Both are solid.

I couldn't independently reproduce the **per-area, per-monkey breakdown** (371/305 frontal, 413/385 auditory) from the raw curation log (`data-extraction/doc/spike_log.csv`): filtering that log by "good"-only units, by "good"+"mua", or by all units all gave totals well off from 676/798 in every combination I tried. Several curation scripts (`nneurons_count.m`, `clean_spike_map.m`) reference a `useNeuron` column that doesn't exist in the current version of that CSV, so the true, final neuron-inclusion criterion used to build the 1474-neuron dataset is evidently computed somewhere I couldn't locate (possibly a step run once and not re-run since, or a column dropped from a later CSV export). This isn't evidence of a wrong number — the final dataset size matches the paper exactly — but it's a traceability gap: if a reviewer or collaborator asked "which neurons, and why," the current repo can't fully answer that from first principles.

## Suggested priority for fixes

1. **Fig 2's central claim ("frontal decodes ordinal position/sequence structure better than auditory")** — resolved, negatively, at the framing level, not just the statistics level, and now via three independent methods (rigorous trial-level LDA, cross-sequence-group generalization, RSA multiple regression). The categorical position/identity decoding claim (Fig 2C-D) does not survive a structural cross-sequence-group control at all (chance-level in both areas; identity decoding at position 5 is structurally inseparable from sequence-group decoding regardless). The continuous trajectory/linearity claim (Fig 2A/B) survives in much-reduced form — a real, significant effect in frontal against a shuffled-time null, but roughly 4x smaller than the reported R²=0.76, and not significantly different from auditory (p=0.667). The RSA regression additionally found a confound-controlled identity signal that is significantly *stronger in auditory than frontal* (p<0.001) — opposite to the paper's claim — and that frontal cortex's one genuine unique advantage, once position and identity are controlled for, is a small residual sequence-group/context effect (p=0.040), not ordinal or item coding. No analysis run in this review supports a frontal-specific ordinal-position advantage once the sequence-context confound is controlled for; if anything, frontal looks distinguished by context/state persistence, not by superior sequence-structure coding. This affects the abstract, discussion, and title framing and needs to be addressed before anything else in the paper.
2. Fig 3 clustering chi-square (proportions vs. counts) — almost certainly a bug, easy fix, re-run and update the reported χ²/p.
3. Fig 4 timescale bootstrap CI (resample neurons, not just trials) — redo the CI properly given how close it is to the significance threshold.
4. Fig 3 GLM: either switch to the described `anova`-on-joint-model approach, apply proper within-condition z-scoring, or update the Methods text to describe what the code actually does (separate `fitglm` models + FDR correction).
5. Document/trace the final neuron-inclusion criterion (`useNeuron` logic) so the 676/798 breakdown is reproducible from raw data.
6. **Resolved:** the sequence-context confound found in the Fig 2 re-analysis (frontal 89% vs auditory 67% decoding of "upcoming" sequence identity from a physically identical stimulus) is trial-N−1 carryover, not anticipation — directly confirmed by decoding the *previous* trial's group from the same window (auditory 0.854, frontal 0.985, both exceeding the current-trial decode). Session/monkey balance and within-session drift were both independently ruled out first. This is itself a genuine, unreported finding (frontal cortex carries trial context substantially longer than auditory cortex, echoing the Fig 4 timescale result) but means none of the whole-trial/sequence-onset-anchored decoding numbers above can be read as stimulus-identity tuning without controlling for trial history.
7. Lower priority: `get_xcond_pca.m` multi-condition intersection, Procrustes 4th pair, dead/duplicate script cleanup (`_old`, `_control`, `2`, `untitled3.m` files) to remove ambiguity about which script produced which figure.
